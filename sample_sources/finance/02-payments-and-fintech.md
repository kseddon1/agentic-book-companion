# Finance Sector Notes: Payments and Fintech

Payment processors sit between merchants, card networks, and banks, and
their reported volumes are a reasonable proxy for overall consumer
spending even when the headline economic data lags. A pickup or
slowdown in processed transaction volume tends to show up in payments
company earnings calls before it shows up in a retail-sales release a
few weeks later.

Fintech lenders that extend credit directly, rather than just moving
payments between other parties, carry a different kind of exposure:
their results depend on how accurately they priced risk when rates
were different than they are today. A sector report covering this
corner of financials should note funding cost trends and loan-loss
provisioning levels as the two figures worth tracking quarter to
quarter, since both are leading indicators of how a lender's book will
perform as the cycle continues.

Regulatory attention on this sub-sector has focused on disclosure and
data-sharing standards between banks and the fintech companies that
plug into their rails, rather than on any single company's specific
business model. That is a sector-level theme, not a company-specific
one, and belongs in the same report as the payments-volume commentary
above rather than in a separate document.
